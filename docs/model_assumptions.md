# Model assumptions

## Beam branch

- Each curved strut is represented by a sequence of B31 beam elements.
- The circular cross-section radius comes from `radius_mm`.
- The model is efficient and suitable for stiffness, force-displacement, stress-strain, and energy-type labels.
- Local 3D von Mises stress fields are not equivalent to solid-element stress fields.

## Swept-solid branch

- Each curved strut should be generated by sweeping a circular profile along the centerline.
- Junctions require careful merging, Boolean union, tie constraints, or connector treatment.
- The model is slower but closer to real 3D printed geometry.

## Voxel branch comparison

- Voxel solid models are easy to automate and naturally suited to 3D CNN input.
- High-resolution 4x4x4 voxel models are expensive because the grid becomes extremely large.
- Voxel boundaries are stair-stepped and may not match smooth printed struts.
